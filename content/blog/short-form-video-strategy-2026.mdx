Short-form video is now the default way people discover brands. But most businesses treat it like a lottery: post something, hope it goes viral, repeat. The brands that win treat it like a system.

Here's the framework we use with clients to turn reels and shorts into a predictable source of enquiries.

## 1. Start with buying questions, not trends

Trends get views. Buying questions get customers. Before you plan a single video, list the 20 questions prospects ask before they buy — on sales calls, in DMs, in reviews. Each one is a video.

Good sources:

- Sales and support call notes
- Competitor reviews (especially 3-star ones)
- Comments on your own and competitors' posts
- Search suggestions on Google and YouTube

## 2. Use four repeatable formats

Inventing a new format every week is exhausting. Pick four that fit your brand and rotate them:

1. **The answer** — one question, answered clearly in under 40 seconds.
2. **The proof** — a result, before/after or customer story.
3. **The process** — behind the scenes of how your product or service works.
4. **The opinion** — a strong, useful point of view on a common mistake in your industry.

## 3. Win the first two seconds

The hook decides everything. Open with the outcome, a surprising number or a direct callout ("If you run a D2C brand, stop doing this"). Put on-screen text in the first frame — most people watch with the sound off.

## 4. Batch production

Plan a month of scripts, then shoot them in one or two sessions. Batching cuts production time dramatically and keeps quality consistent.

## 5. Measure what matters

Views are a leading indicator, not the goal. Track:

- **Saves and shares** — signals of genuine value
- **Profile visits and link clicks** — signals of intent
- **Enquiries from social** — tracked with UTM links and GA4 events

Review monthly, double down on the formats driving enquiries, and retire the rest.
